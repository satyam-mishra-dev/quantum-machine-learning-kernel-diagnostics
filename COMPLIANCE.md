# Problem-Statement Compliance Audit

Line-by-line mapping of the SIH problem statement (Egreen Quanta · Software ·
MedTech/BioTech/HealthTech) to what QuantumFloor (package `qnidaan`) actually
ships. Every number is copied from `runs/*.json`; nothing is projected.

Status legend: ✅ shipped and measured · 🔶 landing tonight (in active build)
· 🗺 roadmap (deliberately after MVP).

## Background

| PS text (verbatim) | Our answer | Status |
|---|---|---|
| "Early and accurate detection of diseases significantly improves treatment outcomes and reduces healthcare costs." | Every model is scored on early-detection-relevant metrics, not just accuracy: AUROC, and specificity at a fixed 95% sensitivity (`spec_at_95sens` in every `runs/*.json` heldout block — e.g. Cleveland PQK 0.781, WDBC PQK 0.986). The metric policy is "never miss a sick patient, then minimize false alarms." | ✅ |
| "Classical machine learning models have achieved notable success in medical diagnosis; however, they often face limitations when dealing with high-dimensional, noisy, and complex biomedical data (e.g., genomics, medical imaging, and electronic health records)." | High-dimensional handled by the Qubit Budgeter (`qnidaan/budgeter.py`): 30-feature WDBC and 13-feature Cleveland compress to 4 qubits with measured explained variance (0.798 / 0.561). Medical imaging: hybrid QNN on PneumoniaMNIST (784-pixel X-rays), AUROC 0.939 (`runs/pneumoniamnist.json`). Genomics: CuMiDa microarray track (thousands of genes → qubit budget). EHR-style tabular data via the universal CSV adapter (ILPD, heart failure, Parkinson's — `runs/adapter_validation.json`); arbitrary EHR/DICOM ingestion is explicitly out of MVP scope (see README "What we deliberately do not build"). | ✅ tabular+imaging · 🔶 genomics (CuMiDa) |
| "Quantum machine learning (QML) offers the potential to capture intricate patterns through quantum superposition and entanglement." | Measured, not asserted: on the quantum-native dataset QSVM hits 0.972 AUROC vs 0.743 best classical twin (`runs/quantum_synth.json`), and at n=30 training rows QSVM 0.826 vs SVM-RBF 0.549. Entanglement depth is tuned per dataset (`qnidaan/tune.py`, `runs/tuned.json`: reps=1 on WDBC/Cleveland, reps=2 on Pima/synthetic). | ✅ |
| "Due to current hardware constraints, a hybrid quantum-classical approach provides a practical pathway to leverage quantum advantages while remaining executable on existing quantum simulators and near-term quantum devices." | All training runs on simulators; inference verified on a real 156-qubit IBM Heron QPU (ibm_marrakesh, job `da8724bsq5js73bko9eg`, 4 qubits used, 1024 shots, 4/4 simulator-hardware agreement, `runs/hardware_cleveland.json`). Circuits stay ≤10 qubits by design (upload cap in `qnidaan/app.py`) so everything is near-term executable. | ✅ |

Classical baselines: matched twins (logreg/SVM-RBF/RF at the same qubit budget) plus a full-feature classical floor in every run. | ✅ |

## Objectives

| PS text (verbatim) | Our answer | Status |
|---|---|---|
| "Design a hybrid quantum-classical machine learning architecture suitable for early disease detection." | Architecture: classical ingestion → Qubit Budgeter → Advantage Scout (pre-training go/no-go, `qnidaan/scout.py`) → quantum models + matched classical twins → trust layer (conformal + SHAP) → report. The Scout is the novel piece: it predicts before training whether quantum is worth trying, validated against measured outcomes on every dataset. | ✅ |
| "Develop quantum-enhanced classification/regression models that can process high-dimensional biomedical data." | Classification: QSVM/PQK/VQC/hybrid-QNN across 9 datasets. Regression: VQR (variational quantum regressor) + PQK-ridge vs classical twins on the sklearn diabetes progression dataset (442 samples, continuous target) — `qnidaan/regression.py`, results JSON landing tonight. High-dimensional: 784-pixel images and 30-feature tabular data through the budgeter. | ✅ classification · 🔶 regression run |
| "Improve detection accuracy, sensitivity, and specificity compared with classical machine learning baselines." | Honest, measured phrasing — never a blanket claim: parity at equal capacity on most clinical tabular data (WDBC 0.996 vs 0.996), measured advantage where data structure supports it (quantum-native: +0.23 AUROC; PneumoniaMNIST: hybrid QNN 0.939 AUROC / 94.6% sensitivity vs matched CNN 0.925 / 90.8%; Cleveland: PQK 0.965 vs 0.961 best classical, and best spec@95%sens 0.781). Sensitivity/specificity reported per model via `spec_at_95sens` and image-track sensitivity/specificity fields. This framing matches the largest independent benchmark (arXiv:2604.18837: 970 experiments, 0/29 significant QSVM wins over classical) — a platform that measures per dataset is the defensible answer. | ✅ |
| "Ensure the platform is scalable, interpretable, and compatible with near-term quantum hardware and simulators." | Scalable: Qubit Budgeter compresses any width to a fixed budget; upload caps (10 qubits / 5MB / 600 rows, caps reported in results); worst-case upload-to-verdict 2.0 min on unseen data (`runs/adapter_validation.json`); cloud deploy on Render (`render.yaml`). Interpretable: SHAP per-patient top features in original clinical names (glucose/age/insulin/bmi on Pima after auto-choice selection). Hardware-compatible: real-QPU receipt (job `da8724bsq5js73bko9eg`) + hardware-readiness score per model landing tonight. | ✅ core · 🔶 readiness score |
| "Incorporate data pre-processing, feature selection, and model explainability modules." | Pre-processing: standardization + budgeting (`qnidaan/budgeter.py`). Feature selection: mutual-info top-k vs PCA, auto-chosen per dataset by CV probe, runner-up score recorded (`runs/compress_exp.json`: selection lifted Pima models ~0.07 AUROC). Explainability: `qnidaan/report.py` — SHAP surrogate, split conformal prediction sets with "flag for human review" on borderline patients, uncalibrated-probability caution flags. | ✅ |
| "Benchmark the hybrid approach against classical models in terms of accuracy, computational efficiency, and generalization performance." | Accuracy: AUROC/accuracy/spec@95sens tables in every `runs/*.json`. Computational efficiency: `train_seconds` per model in every run (Cleveland: PQK 0.17s vs QSVM 15.55s; Pima: PQK 0.47s vs QSVM 133.73s — PQK is ~100-280x cheaper because it needs N circuit evals, not N²); circuit depth/diagram endpoint landing tonight. Generalization: single reserved held-out test split, 5-seed leak-free sample-efficiency curves (whole pipeline refit per subset), conformal coverage. | ✅ tables · 🔶 circuit viz |
